Practice aid based on Parts 3 and 4 of Canada's Transportation of Dangerous Goods Regulations for road transport. Loads cover classes 2.1, 2.2, 3, 4.1, 4.3, 5.1, 6.1, 8 and 9 in non-ERAP quantities. It doesn't cover explosives, toxic gases, radioactives, ERAP loads or air, rail and marine rules. Always check the current TDG Regulations and your company's procedures. All companies, people and phone numbers are made up.

Rules reference

A summary of what this app checks. The TDG Regulations are the legal text.

Shipping document must show

  • Consignor's name and address in Canada
  • Date the document was prepared or given to the carrier
  • The description of each dangerous good, in this order: UN number, shipping name, primary class, subsidiary class(es) in brackets, packing group
  • The UN number with the letters “UN” in front, for example UN1203
  • The proper shipping name from Schedule 1, not a trade name
  • A technical name in brackets after the shipping name when special provision 16 applies (most N.O.S. entries)
  • Packing group in Roman numerals, which may be preceded by “PG”: II or PG II. Gases and some class 9 goods have no packing group.
  • Quantity in SI units (kg or L)
  • Number of small means of containment that need labels
  • A 24-hour number where someone can be reached at any time. CANUTEC's number only if the consignor is registered with CANUTEC or has its permission.
  • ERAP number and phone number when an ERAP is required (not used in this app)
  • The consignor's certification statement and the name of the person who certified it

Where the document goes

  • While driving: in a pocket on the driver's door, or within the driver's reach
  • When you leave the vehicle: in the driver's door pocket, on the driver's seat, or somewhere clearly visible to anyone entering through the driver's door

Placards on a trailer or tank

  • Primary class placard for each dangerous good, on each side and each end
  • Placard sides at least 250 mm
  • 500 kg exemption: if the total gross mass of dangerous goods in small containers is 500 kg or less, placards aren't needed, except for goods such as class 4.3, class 2.3, class 1, ERAP goods and others listed in section 4.16.1
  • DANGER placard: may replace the placards when two or more dangerous goods needing different placards are loaded in small containers, but not for a class over 1,000 kg gross from one consignor, and not for classes such as 4.3, 2.3 or ERAP goods
  • UN number on placards: required for ERAP goods and for a liquid or gas in direct contact with the large means of containment, such as fuel in a highway tank
  • Subsidiary class placards: only required in specific cases (mainly ERAP goods). A subsidiary class like methanol's 6.1 still goes on the paperwork.
